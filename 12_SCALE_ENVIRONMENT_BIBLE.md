ISSUE01-SCALE-BIBLE-1. Sherif, 2026-10-16 11:37 PM ET: "Also bible everything that matters for the environment and character heights, etc. So no one randomly looks different even if exactly correct yet too small or large for each unique camera perspective and environment situation."

This section sits under **3D LOGIC** (Sherif, 2026-10-16 11:36 PM ET). A figure is a solid body in a solid room. Relative size is part of that body. A correct costume at the wrong size fails review. The halo is light, not an object. It stays a flat circle facing the camera above the helm and is not foreshortened, and it is not part of his height.

**How to read the tables.** LOCKED means a council or owner ruling, or a measurement already written in this repo. The citation is the ruling or the file and line. `PROPOSED, needs council ruling` is not canon. Do not draw a proposed number as if it were locked. The lineup sheet and the turnaround sheet are later art cards. This file does not draw them.

Head units differ. Ra-Thor's head is about 25 cm. The human reference uses about 23 cm per head (175 cm / 7.5). Do not mix the two units in one figure.

## 4. Camera rules

Relative scale stays fixed whatever the angle. Measure the bodies. Do not measure the drama.

| Angle | Camera and horizon | How he reads beside a typical human |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Camera near his knee, looking up. The horizon sits low, across the human's chest or his thigh. | He fills the frame. The human is still 5 ft 9 in. Their eyes still meet his gorget or upper chest if both are standing. The low tilt does not shrink the human further. |
| Eye level | Camera at the human's eyes, which is his gorget or upper chest. The horizon runs through that line. | He is about 1.22 times the human. Neither figure is stretched to make the point. |
| High | Camera above the crest, looking down. The horizon sits high in the frame. | He occupies less of the frame. His head is closer to the lens, so it wants to look too big. Keep the head 1/8.5 of his own height. The human beside him stays 5 ft 9 in. |

Lens, as a full-frame equivalent:

| Lens | Use | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Wide, about 24–35 mm | Establishing shots. More room, more arch, more floor. | Foreground boots look large. After the perspective, he is still 8.5 heads, and the human is still 5 ft 9 in. |
| Normal, about 50 mm | Two-shots and dialogue. | Least distortion. This is the check lens for the gorget line. |
| Tele, about 85–135 mm | Visor close-ups. | Depth compresses. A human standing in the same plane does not become a different height because the lens is long. |

Review fails a figure who is the right costume and the wrong size for the room, the doorway, or the person beside them.
