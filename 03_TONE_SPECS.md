# 03 — Tone Specs

House tone modes for Living Thunder. Each one is an original Ra-Thor house mode, named after our own episodes and sets. A spec describes craft only: pacing, framing, light, dialogue rhythm, and the joke or drama engine. Techniques cannot be owned. These specs are the source of truth for tone.

**Derivation rule.** Future episodes, prompts, and new modes build from these specs and from our own episodes. Do not reach for outside works, titles, characters, studios, or creators as references, and do not write "like X" comparisons in any file or prompt. If a new tone is needed, write a new house spec here first, in our own words, then use it.

All modes inherit `00_FIDELITY_PROTOCOL.md`: canon armor, one-line bubbles, hammer grounded, block bullshit and pass awesome, mercy over cruelty.

---

## Snap mode

The fast, bright, punchy register. A problem shows up, gets sorted, and the page is done before it can get preachy.

- **Pacing and beats.** 4 to 5 panels. Setup, one escalation, one clean reversal, button. Every panel moves the situation forward; no idle panels.
- **Framing and camera.** Bold, readable silhouettes at medium-wide. Straight-on or slight low angle on Ra-Thor so he reads as planted. Hard cut between panels, no transitional shots.
- **Lighting and palette.** Flat, high-key gold against obsidian, emerald on the eye-seal, one electric accent for the impact frame. Shapes over texture.
- **Dialogue rhythm.** One line per bubble, two to four words where possible. The reversal panel gets the shortest line on the page.
- **Mechanism.** Speed and certainty. The joke is how little effort the sort takes: the bad thing arrives big, the answer arrives small.
- **What it is NOT.** Not frantic. Not loud for its own sake. Not a beat-down; nobody is flattened for the laugh. No sugar-coating either: the block is a real block.
- **Example beats (our episodes).**
  - E01 Panel 2: a slide-deck creature crumples into chart confetti on the shield. "Blocked."
  - E01 Panel 5: visor only, caption HAMMER STILL GROUNDED. "Next."
  - E03 Panel 2: the paper dart folds itself into a thank-you note. "Patched."

## Lab Night mode

Cartoon-lab gag timing in the Recycler Bay. Machines misbehave, councils bicker, Ra-Thor stays perfectly still.

- **Pacing and beats.** 4 to 6 panels. Hold, hiccup, hold, payoff. The machine gets a beat of its own (a hiccup, a puff of steam, a stuck card) before anyone reacts.
- **Framing and camera.** Wide on the machine so the hopper, drum, and card slot read in one frame; then tight inserts on the cards. Ra-Thor often at frame edge, motionless, as the straight man.
- **Lighting and palette.** Workshop glow: gold circuit walls, warm panel lights, white steam. Emerald for the eye-seal and the good card. One electric accent for the drum fault.
- **Dialogue rhythm.** One line per bubble. Council bubbles are four words. Ra-Thor speaks last and least, often just reading the third card.
- **Mechanism.** Contrast between chaos and stillness, and the rule of three: obvious, feral, clean. The laugh comes from the feral option trying its luck and the stillness not moving.
- **What it is NOT.** Not real science. No reagents, procedures, or equipment detail. Not mad-scientist cruelty, no explosions that hurt anyone, no mocking the guest.
- **Example beats (our episodes).**
  - E02 Panel 2: the drum hiccups, the wolf card appears. Council: "Not the wolf."
  - E02 Panel 4: the wolf card tries to climb back into the hopper. "Recycle it."
  - E05 Panel 5: card three matches what the first mask said. "Let it cool."

## Night Watch silhouette

The pose-and-shadow register for establishing shots and closers. The armor alone says who is on watch.

- **Pacing and beats.** Used for one or two panels per episode, not a full episode by itself: the opener that sets the threshold, or the closer that confirms it held.
- **Framing and camera.** Full figure or three-quarter, low angle, against the gold lattice arch or a dark doorway. Wings and halo ring break the skyline. Cape dark and heavy. Negative space around the figure.
- **Lighting and palette.** Backlight and rim light. Obsidian mass, gold edges, the visor slit as the brightest small point. Emerald only on the eye-seal.
- **Dialogue rhythm.** One short bubble or none. The caption carries the context.
- **Mechanism.** Presence. Stillness reads as strength; the hammer resting head-down on stone says the watch never needed it.
- **What it is NOT.** Not brooding self-pity. Not menace aimed at civilians. Not a fight pose: no raised hammer, no lunge, no chase.
- **Example beats (our episodes).**
  - E01 Panel 1: THRESHOLD. NIGHT. SORT IN PROGRESS. "Left is noise. Right can live."
  - E03 Panel 3: Ra-Thor has not raised the hammer. "Edge held."
  - E06 Panel 1: the Board is awake, nobody speaks, hammer head-down on stone.

## Quiet Board mode

Quiet strategy in a dossier voice. A feed, a choice, consequences shown with restraint.

- **Pacing and beats.** 5 to 6 panels. Observe, detect, deliberate, choose, act, after. The decision panel is slower than the action panel.
- **Framing and camera.** Feed tiles and inserts; over-the-shoulder toward screens; tight on hands and the visor at the moment of choice. Faces of antagonists stay hidden.
- **Lighting and palette.** One practical lamp, deep shadow, the feed wall as the main light source. Gold tiles, one tile in the electric accent when something is wrong.
- **Dialogue rhythm.** One line per bubble, often silent bubbles. Strategy lives in small, clipped, all-caps captions written as case notes (TILE SEVEN. THRESHOLD FEED.).
- **Mechanism.** Tension through restraint. The audience sees the choice before the action, and the payoff is a block that is smaller and kinder than it could have been.
- **What it is NOT.** Not real surveillance. No readable personal data, real places, or real faces on any feed. No hacking steps. Not paranoia for its own sake.
- **Example beats (our episodes).**
  - E06 Panel 2: tile seven shows a gift rolling toward the wrong lane.
  - E06 Panel 4: the gauntlet leaves the hammer and reaches for the shield. THE HAMMER IS AN OPTION. MERCY IS A BETTER ONE.
  - E06 Panel 6: "Blocked. Door's still open."

## Suave Hour mode

Spy-thriller elegance with the armor on. A negotiation where the line does the work.

- **Pacing and beats.** 5 to 6 panels. Set the table, opening line, counter, the offer, the block, the clean close. Every exchange is a volley of one line each.
- **Framing and camera.** Two-shots across a table, over-the-shoulder on the offer, tight on gloves, cards, and glasses. Low, level camera; no frantic angles.
- **Lighting and palette.** One practical, deep shadow, lamp glint on gold filigree. Crystal and cards catch the light. Emerald reserved for the eye-seal.
- **Dialogue rhythm.** One line per bubble, dry and slightly fond. A line that could cut both ways. The best line is usually the reply, not the opener.
- **Mechanism.** Poise under temptation. The counterpart offers something with strings; Ra-Thor stays composed, blocks it, and still leaves a fair deal on the table. Spice ceiling is a raised eyebrow.
- **What it is NOT.** Not seduction. Not gadget worship, gunplay, or violence as style. The armor does not come off. No real people, no recognizable characters.
- **Example beats (our episodes).**
  - E04 Panel 1–2: "Most guests take the armor off for the hour." / "Most guests lose the hour."
  - E04 Panel 4: the shield pins the EXCLUSIVE card; gold strings go slack. "Blocked."
  - E04 Panel 6: TWO GLASSES. NEITHER TOUCHED. "That one can live."

## Mixing modes

An episode has one lead mode. It may borrow a single panel from another (usually a Night Watch silhouette opener or closer). Future episodes name their lead mode in the status line.
