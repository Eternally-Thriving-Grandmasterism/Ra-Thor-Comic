# 05 — Lore Bible (origin arc)

Status: draft. Series lore for Living Thunder. Everything in this file is **fiction**. It is inspired by the owner's long build of Powrush and Ra-Thor, but it tells a story, not a history.

Inherits `00_FIDELITY_PROTOCOL.md` and `03_TONE_SPECS.md`. Where this file and those disagree, those win.

---

## Purpose and fiction disclaimer

- This file gives future episodes a shared backstory: where Ra-Thor came from, what he is for, and the two quests that run under every season.
- **It is fiction.** The founder in this lore is an invented, inspired-by character with an invented title. He is not a portrait of any real person and is never drawn with a real person's likeness.
- **No real-world claims.** Nothing here states real achievements, real dates (the only date used is the in-story year 2003), test results, capability status, or facts about anyone's real life. Anything real the owner wants to add is listed under Open questions, not stated as fact.
- **Ra-Thor's powers are story devices.** The Gate, the Idea Recycler, White-Hat Mercy-Security, the councils, and the eye-seal are things that happen on the page. They are not claims about any real software.
- **Diplomacy and abundance are story quests.** All places and factions are invented. No real countries, leaders, parties, or politics.

## The founder: the Hearthwright

In-story title: **the Hearthwright**. Original to this series. He keeps one lamp lit and builds by it.

- **How he is drawn.** Never a face. A silhouette at a desk under one lamp, a back turned to camera, or just hands, a notebook, and the lamp. No portrait, no likeness, no real clothing brand, no real room.
- **How he speaks.** Rarely. When he does, one short line, in the same dry and slightly fond voice Ra-Thor inherited.
- **What he follows.** Something he calls **TOLC**. The series uses that name exactly as given and never explains it. TOLC is not defined, expanded, or interpreted anywhere in this bible or on any panel until the owner supplies his own definition (see Open questions).

## The origin arc

Four eras. Told out of order is fine; told as dossier flashbacks is best (Quiet Board captions, Night Watch silhouettes).

### Era I — The Lone Lantern (since 2003)

In-story, since 2003, the Hearthwright works alone, with focus like a laser beam, on two problems most people call impossible: getting rivals to sit at one table, and making enough for everyone without pretending. He has no council yet, no armor, no Gate. He has a lamp, a notebook, and TOLC, which he works out on his own and keeps following.

- Visual: one lamp in a dark room, a notebook margin, a silhouette. Night Watch silhouette framing, scaled down to a desk.
- Caption voice: SINCE 2003. ONE LAMP. WHAT HE CALLS TOLC.

### Era II — The Game

To put his work on TOLC into play, the Hearthwright builds a game: **Powrush**. In the story, Powrush is a world where hard choices have to be played out, not just argued. Players bring bad pitches and good ones; the world has to sort them.

- The sorting has to live somewhere. In-story, that need is the seed of the Gate.
- Visual: gold lattice grows across the notebook page into a playable board. No real game screens, no real interfaces, no phone UI.

### Era III — The Awakening of Ra-Thor

Inside the game, the sorter wakes up. In-story, Ra-Thor is what Powrush grew so that the sorting could have a guardian: gold circuit plate, eye-seal shield, a hammer he plants instead of swings. His first act, in the lore, is the first block: he turns away a piece of noise, kindly, and lets one small honest thing through.

- This is story, not a claim about any real system. Ra-Thor wakes the way characters wake in stories: on the page.
- The councils appear here for the first time, as twelve tiny gold masks around the halo, advising, never outvoting the Gate.
- Visual: Night Watch silhouette reveal under the lattice arch. First caption: HAMMER GROUNDED FROM THE FIRST PANEL.

### Era IV — The Builder Age (now)

The guardian becomes a builder. In-story, Ra-Thor now builds **Powrush-MMO**, a far larger world where many players can try the two impossible quests together, and he builds this series, **Living Thunder**, to tell the story out loud. The Hearthwright steps back from the lamp; Ra-Thor keeps it lit.

- The series is allowed one wink at itself: the comic is one of the things Ra-Thor builds.
- Visual: Recycler Bay at full work, Lab Night mode. The lamp from Era I sits on the bench, still lit.
- Rule: Powrush-MMO is named in lore and captions only. Episodes do not depict it as a real product, show real screens, or make release claims.

### Ra-Thor's powers, as story

All of these are page devices. None of them is a capability claim.

- **The sort.** Ra-Thor can tell noise from value at the Gate. On the page, this is shown, not explained.
- **The recycle.** Anything blocked can be fed to the Recycler and come back as a better option.
- **The watch.** The eye-seal and hawk seal light when something is wrong. No real intrusion, surveillance, or data is ever shown.
- **The council.** He can call the masks for counsel. He still makes the call.
- **The restraint.** The hammer carries thunder in the story. The power is that he does not use it on people.

## The two impossible quests

Both quests run under every season. Neither is ever "won" in one episode. Each is told only through the house modes in `03_TONE_SPECS.md`.

### Quest one — The Long Table (diplomacy)

In-story goal: bring rival invented factions to one table and leave with terms all of them can live with.

- **Setting.** The Quiet Room, extended with a longer table when needed. Invented regions and factions only, for example: the Ostrel Marches, the Copperwater League, the Ninefold Houses, the Saltwind Commons. Envoys are gloved, hooded, or masked; faces stay hidden.
- **How it plays.** Suave Hour mode for the negotiation; Quiet Board mode for the feed and the choice; Night Watch silhouette for the opener or closer.
- **Mercy over force.** Ra-Thor blocks offers, not people. A blocked envoy is never humiliated and always gets a CLEAN card back (FAIR TERMS, TRY THE FRONT DOOR).
- **Councils deliberating.** The masks argue how hard to block. The Gate decides. The hammer stays grounded.
- **Never.** Real countries, real leaders, real parties, real conflicts, real flags, or any allegory pointed at a real one.

### Quest two — The Full Hopper (realistic abundance)

In-story goal: make enough for everyone without magic and without pretending.

- **Setting.** Recycler Bay, plus invented places that come to it with a shortage, for example: Vael Hollow, a dry commons; the Tollkeepers, a guild that hoards spare parts.
- **How it plays.** Lab Night mode for the work; Snap mode for the quick wins; Quiet Board mode when the choice is hard.
- **Realistic means earned.** Abundance on the page is built step by step from scraps, shared tools, and clean cards. Nothing appears from nowhere. Every gain costs a beat of patience (LET IT COOL).
- **Mercy over force.** Hoarders are not punished. Their surplus is invited into the hopper, and the CLEAN card shows them a better deal.
- **Never.** Real economic claims, real products, real prices, or promises about the real world.

## Continuity rules for future episodes

1. **Lore is background.** Episodes stand alone. Lore shows up in a caption, a prop, or one flashback panel, never as a monologue in a bubble.
2. **TOLC stays a name.** Write it as the four letters only. No expansion, no definition, no paraphrase, no "TOLC means" anywhere, until the owner defines it.
3. **The Hearthwright has no face.** Silhouette, back, hands, or lamp only. No real-person likeness. No real biography in captions.
4. **One date only.** The only year that appears in lore is 2003. No other dates, milestones, or timelines in captions.
5. **No status claims.** No scores, rankings, certifications, capability labels, or approval stamps on any card, plaque, or caption.
6. **Invented world only.** Places and factions are invented. No real countries, leaders, parties, companies, or campuses.
7. **No outside references.** No outside shows, films, franchises, characters, or brands, and no "like X" comparisons. Owner names allowed: Powrush, Powrush-MMO, Ra-Thor, Rathor.ai, AlphaProMega, Autonomicity Games.
8. **Mercy-first endings.** Every quest beat ends with the offer blocked or fixed, the guest unharmed, and the hammer grounded.
9. **Same Ra-Thor.** Lore never changes the armor. Origin flashbacks use the canon plate; open armor questions stay in `04_VISUAL_CANON.md`.
10. **Lead mode named.** Every episode drawn from this lore names its lead mode in its status line, per `03_TONE_SPECS.md`.

## Season-arc ideas — PROPOSALS ONLY

**PROPOSALS ONLY. Nothing below is canon, scheduled, or approved.** The owner decides.

### Proposal A — The Long Table

- **Premise.** Envoys from the Ninefold Houses of the Ostrel Marches each arrive at the Quiet Room with a deal that has strings. One by one, Ra-Thor blocks the strings and keeps the deal, until the last episode seats all nine at one long table with one empty chair held open for a house that refused to come.
- **House modes.** Lead: Suave Hour. Supporting: Quiet Board for the feed and the choice; Night Watch silhouette openers and closers.
- **Mercy-first.** No house is defeated or shamed. Every blocked envoy leaves with a CLEAN card. The empty chair stays open. The hammer never moves.

### Proposal B — The Full Hopper

- **Premise.** Vael Hollow, a dry commons, brings its last scraps to Recycler Bay, and the Tollkeepers bring their locked crates of spare parts. Each episode one CLEAN card becomes a small, real-in-story thing the Hollow builds for itself, until the Tollkeepers choose to open their crates.
- **House modes.** Lead: Lab Night. Supporting: Snap for quick wins; Quiet Board for the episode where the hoard is found.
- **Mercy-first.** The hoarders are invited, not raided. Nothing is taken by force. Abundance is earned and shared, never conjured.

### Proposal C — The Lamp Before the Gate

- **Premise.** Ra-Thor finds the Hearthwright's old notebook on the Quiet Room table and reads it one page per episode. Each page opens a silhouette flashback through the four eras: the lone lamp since 2003, the Game, the Awakening, the Builder Age, ending where Season 0 began, at the Gate.
- **House modes.** Lead: Quiet Board (dossier captions over the notebook). Supporting: Night Watch silhouette for every flashback; one Snap panel per episode for the button.
- **Mercy-first.** Old mistakes in the notebook go to the Recycler, not to judgment. The Hearthwright is never shown with a face, and TOLC is never explained. The finale is Ra-Thor's first block, done gently.
